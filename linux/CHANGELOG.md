# Changelog

All notable changes to BetterShot for Linux are documented in this file. The
macOS app keeps its own [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).
Each release gets a `## [x.y.z]` section matching `version.json`; the app shows
that section as What's New after an update.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Screenshots on Linux.** Capture an area from a still frame of the screen, every display, a window through your desktop's screenshot dialog, or the last area again, with an optional countdown. Captures stay private until you save them; new installs save region, screen, and window screenshots to `Pictures/Screenshots` automatically.
- **The capture deck.** Each capture gets a card with Copy, Save, Edit, Pin, Share, and Dismiss. Drag a card into another app to hand over the file, or double-click it to edit. Cards hide after a few seconds unless the pointer is over them.
- **The image editor.** Rectangles, circles, lines, arrows, freehand, numbered circles, text, highlight, pixelate, and blur, plus crop, rotate, and flip, with undo. Frame captures with gradients, colors, or images, padding, corners, shadow, and a watermark. Save keeps edits editable in the macOS `.bettershot` format and updates the saved file; Copy and Export As never change it.
- **Capture Text and Pick Color.** Drag over text to copy it, using Tesseract, with QR codes read first when `zbarimg` is installed; an area without text leaves the clipboard alone. Pick Color uses your desktop's color picker, or a magnifier where the desktop has none, and copies `#RRGGBB`.
- **Smart Redaction.** The editor finds email addresses, links, IP addresses, phone and card numbers, tokens, and labeled secrets, and covers them with Pixelate or Blur in one step.
- **Pinned screenshots.** Keep a capture on screen in a borderless window you can move, scale with the scroll wheel, copy, and close.
- **Scrolling capture.** Select an area, then add a frame after each scroll; BetterShot stitches them into one tall image and keeps a fixed header once.
- **Cloud sharing with your own Cloudflare R2 bucket.** Share from a card or the editor to upload a compressed copy with a poster and copy a link to a viewer page or the file itself. Set it up in Settings › Sharing; keys are stored in your login keyring.
- **The Media Gallery.** Browse captures on this computer and your cloud shares, search and sort them, and open, edit, copy links, move captures to the trash, or delete shares, each confirmed first.
- **First-run setup and What's New.** A four-step setup checks screenshot access and shortcuts, chooses where screenshots go, and walks through a first capture. Settings › About reopens it and these notes.
- **Screen recording.** Record a display, a window, or an area from the capture bar (Super+Shift+2, or `bettershot record`): your desktop’s screen-sharing dialog picks the display or window, an area is cropped from its display, and the recording bar shows the time with Pause, Start Over, and Discard, the last two asking first. System audio and a microphone are separate tracks, a camera records beside the screen, and a countdown and the frame rate are in Settings › Recording. Each recording gets a card with its first frame and length: Save writes an MP4 to your folder (or automatically), Copy, Open, and Delete. Recordings are stored as projects in the macOS app’s package format, playable up to the last second even after a crash; the next launch recovers interrupted ones into the Media Gallery.
- **The video studio.** Edit a recording from its card, the Media Gallery, or `bettershot edit <project>`: the inspector on the left sets the background, padding, corners, and shadow (new recordings start from General › Default Look), the camera bubble's layout, side, size, roundness, and frame, a crop, blur and pixelate masks with time ranges, zooms with magnification and focus, and the export format, frame rate, quality, resolution, and audio. The timeline cuts with Scissors (S), deletes clips, changes their speed, and shows removed durations; Space plays the preview. Undo and redo cover every edit, drafts save while you work, Save re-renders the project and replaces the saved video, and Export As writes a new one. Projects use the macOS app's format, so they open on either platform.
- **Cursor styles and click effects.** Recordings made in an X11 session hide the system cursor and keep the pointer, its clicks, and keyboard shortcuts (never plain typing) beside the footage, as the macOS app does. The studio's Cursor section then shows or hides the cursor, restyles it as Recorded, Arrow, Dark, Light, or Dot, sizes it, smooths its motion, adds press and ripple click effects, and hides it when idle; macOS projects with pointer data get the same treatment. Saving such a recording from its card renders it with your default look. Wayland sessions keep the cursor in the picture.
- **Handles on the studio preview.** Drag the camera bubble where you want it, resize or move a blur or pixelate mask by its handles, and Adjust Crop to drag the crop over the whole recording (Return applies, Escape cancels and keeps the previous crop). Every drag is one undo step.
- **Reframing and motion blur.** Export › Aspect ratio renders a recording as 16:9, 16:10, 4:3, 9:16, 1:1, or 4:5: Fill crops into it along a calm camera that follows your cursor and hands over to your zooms, Fit frames the whole recording on the background. A crop now sets the output size, as on macOS, and exports blur the picture while the camera moves.
- **Captions, presets, and sound in the studio.** Keyboard shortcuts recorded on X11 appear as captions over the video, placed in any corner or edge, and projects transcribed by the macOS app show their subtitle bar with the spoken word highlighted. The Background section's Presets menu saves the current look under a name and applies or deletes saved looks. The preview now plays the recording's sound, following cuts and clip speeds. Recording projects on disk that the library does not list yet appear in the Media Gallery.
- **The teleprompter.** The capture bar's Script button opens the script window; while recording, the prompter shows the script near the top of the screen and scrolls a line at a time with the wheel, the arrow keys, or its buttons.
- **Narration subtitles with whisper.cpp.** With the `whisper-cli` command installed and a ggml model chosen in Settings › Recording, the studio's Transcribe button turns the narration into timed subtitles, chunked at pauses as on macOS, with the spoken word highlighted.
- **3D shots.** Fly a camera around the recording with Cap's thirteen moves (Glide across, Rising sweep, Pull back, Top down, …) or let Auto Scene lay out a sequence; each shot has its timing, easing, transitions, Reverse, and a depth blur with bokeh. Shots from macOS projects play back the same, keyframes included.
- **Packages.** `make deb`, `make appimage`, and `make flatpak` build a Debian package, an AppImage, and a Flatpak.
- **Desktop integration.** Global shortcuts through the GlobalShortcuts portal (Super+Shift+4 for an area, as on macOS with Super for ⌘), a tray icon, app-menu actions, `bettershot://` links, and a `bettershot` command for every capture, usable as a custom shortcut where the portal is missing.

### Changed

- **Capture cards are Large by default.** The macOS default, Small, is a 180-point card, which is hard to read on the 1x displays most Linux desktops use. Settings › Cards › Card size still offers Small and Medium, and a size chosen earlier is kept.

### Fixed

- Share links saved with a capture now use the same key as the macOS app (`shareURL`), so a library shared between the two keeps them. Links saved by earlier development builds still load.
- **No more screen flashes on GNOME.** GNOME's screenshot portal flashes the whole screen for every shot it takes, and scrolling capture took up to four per frame. Still frames for region, scrolling, text, and color captures now come silently from a screen-sharing stream (Settings › Capture › Read the screen through screen sharing, on by default on GNOME; your desktop asks once which screens to share and remembers it), a scrolling session keeps one stream open for all its frames, and where the stream is unavailable the screenshot portal is used once per frame. Choosing a recording area and setup's screenshot check use the same path.
- **Silent captures on X11 sessions.** On an Xorg session the still frame is read straight from the X server, so region, scrolling, text, and color captures show no dialog and no flash there, whatever the desktop.
- **Share links and saved recordings reach the clipboard on Wayland.** The link copied after an upload, and a recording's file after an automatic save, are now written once the card or editor window is focused, as the compositor requires.
- **Display recordings remember the picked display.** The ScreenCast choice for display and area recordings is kept across launches, so the picker appears once; window recordings still ask every time, since the window is the point.
- **Capture Text and Pick Color copy on Wayland.** The compositor accepts clipboard writes only from the focused app, and BetterShot had no focused window after the selector or the desktop's picker closed, so the text or color never reached the clipboard. A small Copied window now takes focus and writes it, with a Copy button in case the desktop keeps the focus elsewhere, and the deck copies a capture only once its window is focused.
